AI points
1. Trial room congestion during peak hours (festival season, weekends) — this app effectively adds "virtual trial room capacity."
2. Staff time spent on garment handling (folding, ironing, hanging back up) instead of selling.
3. Customer decision fatigue — physically trying more than 3-4 items is exhausting, so people often buy from a small, arbitrary subset rather than their actual best match.
4. Sales opportunity loss — a customer who's tired from trying 3 sarees won't ask to see 4 more even if the shop has better options; a screen doesn't get tired.
5. Risks & mitigations
Technical risk: generation quality inconsistency, especially on sarees — mitigate with one-tap regenerate, staff-side quality check before showing customer.
Adoption risk: staff reverting to old habits under time pressure — mitigate with training + making the app faster than manual draping, not just "an option."
Margin risk: premium credit overuse — mitigated by your tiered credit system (already solved).
Seasonal cash flow risk — already discussed, worth a line here too.

To add:
1. Data privacy & consent (important gap — customer photos are sensitive)
A written policy for: what happens to a customer's photo after generation (deleted immediately? stored for repeat visits with consent? for how long?).
Explicit customer consent step in the flow — right now the flow says "if they say yes" for the photo, but the document should state what "yes" actually covers (one-time use vs. stored).
A basic awareness note on India's data protection law (DPDP Act) as it applies to storing personal photos — worth a proper legal consult before wide rollout, but flag it in the doc now so it's not forgotten later.
2. Competitive landscape
Who else is doing VTON for Indian fashion or retail generally (there are a few Indian retail-AI players like Vue.ai, Fynd, and others worth checking current positioning on) and how you're different — your saree/complex-drape focus and offline-first staff workflow are your differentiators, so name them explicitly against competitors rather than assuming it's obvious.
Why a shop would pick you over just asking customers to record a video trying clothes, or over doing nothing.
